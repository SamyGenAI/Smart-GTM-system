# Signals

What counts as a signal, and how to rank it.

This file holds rules only. **Who** to watch lives in `watchlist.md`.
Both signal skills read this file to judge what is worth surfacing.

---

## What to surface

**Topics that matter:** [The problem you solve, your category name, pricing complaints, competitor names, hiring pain.]

**Ignore:** [Reposts, generic motivational posts, promos, engagement bait, job-board spam.]

**Makes a post actionable:** [Names a pain you solve, asks for a recommendation, announces a change that opens a window.]

---

## Intent signals

**Hiring a role**
Opens a role that owns the problem you solve, at an ICP-fit company.
Strength: strong — budget exists and the gap is admitted.

**Funding**
Raise at a stage where they build the function you serve.
Strength: medium — budget appears, but the window opens weeks later.

**Job change**
Known contact moves into a buying seat.
Strength: strong — new leaders rebuild their stack in the first 90 days.

**Product launch**
Ships something needing the capability you provide.
Strength: medium — creates a moment worth referencing.

**Engagement**
Someone engages with your posts or a competitor's.
Strength: medium — attention without stated need. Strong when repeated.

**Website visit**
An identified company hits your pricing or case-study pages.
Strength: strong — but needs a deanonymization vendor (RB2B, Vector, Leadfeeder). Not observable from public data.

---

## How to rank

- [Lead with time-sensitive signals, then the rest.]
- [A job change or a relevant role opening outranks a funding round — they imply an owner and an admitted gap, not just money.]
- [Repeated engagement from one person beats any single one-off signal.]
- [A competitor's positioning change is strategic context, not an outreach trigger. It changes the pitch, it does not create a lead.]
- [Anything older than 14 days is background, not news.]
- [Two signals on the same company in one week matter more than either alone — always say so.]
